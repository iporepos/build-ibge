Harmonized dataset from the 1991 Brazilian Demographic Census by IBGE

This dataset provides a harmonized version of the 1991 Brazilian Demographic Census results at the census tract and municipal levels, together with the harmonized sample microdata.

The data are distributed as separate, gzip-compressed files (one per table and per geography layer) together with the script that builds a single GeoPackage from them. Variable names were harmonized, data types and missing-value codes were made consistent, and structural inconsistencies were resolved or documented, to facilitate reproducible analysis and integration with GIS and data science workflows. It follows the structure of the companion datasets for the 2000, 2010 and 2022 censuses.

The original data sources are the 1991 Brazilian Demographic Census conducted by the Instituto Brasileiro de Geografia e Estatística (IBGE): the census tract aggregates of the universe results (IBGE publication DVD "Censo demográfico 1991: agregado por setores: resultados do universo", one spreadsheet per set and state), the sample microdata, the municipal values published by IBGE, the 1991 municipal mesh and municipal seats, the Territorial Division (DTB) of 1991, and the IBGE comparability table between 2022 and 1991 census tracts.

What is in this deposit

Files:

  • tract tables *.csv.gz — basico, domicilio, morador, responsavel, pessoa01, pessoa02, instrucao01, instrucao02. One row per census tract (161,697 tracts), first column setor_code, then the variables V + number (283 variables in total).
  • sample microdata *.csv.gz — microdados-domicilios (4,024,553 households), microdados-pessoas (17,045,712 persons), and microdados-setores, the inferred census tract of each sample household (see below).
  • municipal table *.csv.gz — agregados-municipios, values published by IBGE for the 4,491 municipalities.
  • comparability table *.csv.gz — comparabilidade-2022, the IBGE links between 1991 and 2022 census tracts (Rio Grande do Sul only for now).
  • geospatial layers *.gpkg.gz — municipios, unidades and sedes.
  • metadata catalogs: IBGE_CENSO-1991_LAYERS_X_X.csv, IBGE_CENSO-1991_FIELDS_X_X.csv, IBGE_CENSO-1991_VARIABLES_X_X.csv, IBGE_CENSO-1991_CATEGORIES_X_X.csv, IBGE_CENSO-1991_CONSISTENCY_X_X.csv — the metadata catalogs and the consistency table (uncompressed, readable on the repository).
  • builder script build_database_1991.py and its configuration build_database_1991.json — the script that builds the GeoPackage database from the files above.
  • checksums: IBGE_CENSO-1991_MANIFEST_X_X.csv and IBGE_CENSO-1991_SHA256SUMS_X_X.txt (SHA-256 of every packaged file and of its uncompressed source).

Files are kept separate on purpose, so that new versions of the deposit only replace the files that changed (for example, when IBGE releases the comparability tables of other states). The .csv.gz files can be read directly by pandas, pyarrow, DuckDB, R and most other tools; the .gpkg.gz files must be decompressed before use in GIS software. Uncompressed, the files take about 8 GB, most of it the person microdata (7.3 GB).

Building the database

1. Download all files into one folder and decompress them (gunzip *.gz).
2. From that folder, run python build_database_1991.py --config build_database_1991.json --overwrite.

Requirements: Python 3 with numpy, pandas and pyarrow, the GDAL command line tool ogr2ogr available in the PATH, and about 30 GB of RAM (the person microdata are aggregated in memory). The build takes a few minutes, writes a GeoPackage of about 1 GB to ./database, and writes a log with checks (row counts, key cross-checks, estimates against the universe counts). The header of the script documents every option of the configuration.

Layers

The GeoPackage has 22 layers, named without census or year and with underscores (e.g. basico, agregados_municipios). The microdata are not loaded in the database; they are aggregated into the estimate tables.

  • Geospatial:
      - municipios - the 4,491 municipalities of 1991, with mesoregion, microregion and metropolitan region, plus four special areas (Lagoa dos Patos, Lagoa Mirim, the PI/CE disputed area and islands not assigned to a municipality), 4,495 polygons.
      - unidades - the 27 federative units, aggregated from the municipal mesh.
      - sedes - points of the municipal seats, linked to municipios by municipio_code.
  • Universe tables (values published by IBGE):
      - basico, domicilio, morador, responsavel, pessoa01, pessoa02, instrucao01, instrucao02 - the tract tables, one row per setor_code. basico holds the codes and names of the municipality, district and subdistrict, the tract situation and type, five tract means (head income, income in collective dwellings, rooms, bedrooms and bathrooms per household) and three totals repeated from the other tables (residents, permanent private households and all households, as in the 2000 and 2022 basico); the other tables hold counts (households by type, water supply, sanitation, tenure and garbage; residents by sex, age group and relationship to the head; literacy; heads by schooling and income bracket).
      - agregados_municipios - resident and household totals, sampling fraction, urban and rural population, population by sex and the Gini index published by IBGE, one row per municipio_code.
  • Sample estimates (computed when the database is built, not IBGE values):
      - estimativas_municipios_domicilios, estimativas_municipios_pessoas - the microdata aggregated per municipality, weighted by the household weight.
      - estimativas_setores_domicilios, estimativas_setores_pessoas - the same aggregation per inferred census tract (setor_code_inferred), one row per tract of basico.
    For each categorical variable, V<n>_<code> is the estimated number of households or persons in that category, and V<n>_ign those with an ignored answer; for each quantity (income, rooms...), V<n>_n, V<n>_sum and V<n>_mean are the units with a valid value, the weighted sum and the weighted mean. Every row has n_sample, the number of sample records it is based on (and n_sample_households in the person tables), and total, the sum of the weights.
  • Links: comparabilidade_2022 - pairs setor_code_2022 / setor_code_1991 (many to many), to bring 1991 tract data to the 2022 tract geometry.
  • Five structured metadata layers:
      - layers - Describes all database layers, their theme and content.
      - fields - Documents the attribute fields and naming conventions, including identifiers, geographic codes, missing-value codes and the column patterns of the estimate tables.
      - variables - Lists every variable of every table, with the original IBGE name, the dictionary number and the descriptions in Portuguese and English; in the estimate tables, one row per column with its category label.
      - categories - The labels of the tract situation and tract type codes.
      - consistency - Lists the findings of the harmonization for later revisions (layer, code, issue, measured value, action taken, note).

Together, these metadata layers provide full transparency regarding database structure, schema harmonization, and variable provenance. The IBGE_CENSO-1991_CATEGORIES_X_X.csv file of the deposit also holds the code labels of every categorical variable of the microdata.

Conventions

- Names follow <entity>_code / <entity>_name (e.g. setor_code, municipio_code, municipio_name). Variables are named V + number and identified in variables by variable_code (<table>_<variable>, e.g. domicilio_V0001). The tract variables are numbered V0001–V0275 across the tract tables (V01–V08 in basico), keeping the original DVD mnemonic in source_name; the microdata variables follow the IBGE dictionary number (item 9.32 → V0932).
- Codes: setor_code has 15 digits, municipality (7) + district (2) + subdistrict (2) + tract (4), the form IBGE uses in its comparability tables, so distrito_code and subdistrito_code are its first 9 and 11 digits. The 1991 codes are not comparable with the 2000 codes of the same format. Every column ending in _code is stored as text.
- Microdata: household_code = municipality code + a 6-digit sequence, person_code = household code + person number. Persons link to households by household_code; estimates need the household weight V0927. Booleans are 1/0 and income is in cruzeiros of September 1991.
- Missing values use fixed codes (documented in the fields layer): -9999 no data or not applicable and -2 ignored (the respondent did not know or did not answer). The tract tables have no missing values. Text fields use NA.
- Table names: the files use hyphens (e.g. AGREGADOS-MUNICIPIOS), the database uses underscores (agregados_municipios) so that tables need no quoting in SQL.

Data quality notes

- There is no tract geometry for 1991. The tract tables are linked to the municipalities by municipio_code, and to the 2022 tracts by comparabilidade_2022 where IBGE has released the table (Rio Grande do Sul, 36,924 pairs; other states on request). In Rio Grande do Sul, 66 codes of the IBGE table are absent from the tract tables, and the tracts of four municipalities are missing from it (Pelotas 261, Passo Fundo 139, Novo Hamburgo 4, Gravataí 2); all are listed in consistency.
- The eight tract tables cover the same 161,697 tracts. Their 173 internal identities hold in every tract (e.g. sexes add up to the total, age groups to the population), and the tract totals of population and permanent private households equal the values published per municipality in every municipality. 3,093 exact duplicate rows of the source (Distrito Federal and Rondônia files of the relationship set) were dropped.
- The tract tables only cover the basic questionnaire. Of the 131 microdata variables, 109 (race, religion, migration, fertility, labour, income components, schooling of non-heads, household assets...) exist only in the sample.
- The DVD does not define the tract type codes; tipo_code uses the 2000 labels, which the data support (99% of the households in type 1 tracts are in subnormal agglomerations). District and subdistrict names are kept as written in the DVD (upper case).
- The microdata carry no tract code. microdados-setores rebuilds the tract of each sample household from the record order and from the weights, whose sums match the household counts of the tracts:setor_code_inferred is an inference, not an IBGE code. 99.0% of the tracts receive sample households; the estimated shares of households with piped water and with garbage collection correlate 0.976–0.997 with the universe values in every state. The municipality remains the reliable link of the microdata.
- Sample estimates by tract are based on few records (median 24 households and 96 persons per tract; 1,627 tracts have none). Check n_sample before use, and prefer the universe tables for the items of the basic questionnaire.
- In the microdata, each person in a collective dwelling forms a household of its own, so the estimated households exceed the universe count of permanent private households (35.4 million vs 34.7 million). The estimated population equals the universe within 0.01% (146,815,790 vs 146,825,475).
- The Gini index (V008 of agregados_municipios) is copied from IBGE spreadsheets of 1995; its income concept is not documented and the sample does not reproduce it exactly. 29 municipalities have no value (-9999).
- Municipal seats: the codes of Itapipoca and Amontada were swapped in the source and are fixed; municipalities with more than one point keep the one nearest to the 2000 seat; three points without geometry were dropped.
- Coordinates are in SIRGAS 2000 (EPSG:4674). Names and codes of municipalities come from the DTB 1991, not from the mesh files.
- Descriptions in the variables layer come from the IBGE documentation of the DVD and of the microdata; the English text was translated for this dataset.

basico gains V06–V08: residents, permanent private households and all households of the tract (the same values as pessoa01 V0114, domicilio V0002 and V0001), so that basico holds the main totals as in the other censuses. The comparability table columns are named setor_code_1991 and setor_code_2022. No other value changed.

First version of the 1991 dataset: universe tract tables (without geometry), sample microdata with an inferred tract, municipal values published by IBGE, municipal geography, and the 2022–1991 comparability table of Rio Grande do Sul. Tables and layers are distributed as separate compressed files, and users can rebuild the database by running the build_database_1991.py script.
